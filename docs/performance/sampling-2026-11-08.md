# Initial sampling group budget — 2026-11-08

The initial fixed internal budget is **2,000 eligible sampling groups per
view**. It applies after the fixed filters and uses complete target candidates.
Every retained group's eligible loaded records remain together, while each
record keeps its own drawing and row placement. The 10,000-base visibility
limit remains independent and takes precedence.

The budget follows 60 native release measurements at five candidate budgets,
using the two public HG002 loci from the
[early visibility baseline](visibility-2026-11-07.md). A budget of 2,000 was the
smallest tested choice that preserved all 1,825 eligible groups in the ordinary
1 kb case. In the deeper 1 kb case it reduced the fresh layout from 1,957 to
539 rows, compared with 1,349 rows at 5,000 groups. Its median load was 85.15 ms,
compared with 117.20 ms at 5,000 and 155.55 ms without sampling. This chooses a
practical initial balance between retained evidence and vertical extent; it
does not establish an optimal budget or a hard performance bound.

## Observed tradeoffs

Each row reports medians across three processes. Load includes decoding,
sampling, row packing, presentation callbacks, and native layout. Reader time
is separately recorded in the raw data. Draw is the mean of five actual visible
CPU bitmap rasterizations per process; it excludes allocation, PNG encoding,
compositor presentation, and frame scheduling. Peak RSS covers the entire
process, including startup and exports; it is not incremental alignment memory.

At 2,000 groups, the deeper 10 kb view retained 3,285 records and 159 rows;
all 50,548 queried records still had to be decoded, including 47,160 eligible
records used to determine the complete candidate set. Its median reader time
was 69.75 ms, versus 71.91 ms without sampling. Smaller display samples did not
provide a corresponding reduction in query work or process peak RSS. In the
ordinary 1 kb runs, whole-process RSS varied by about 30 MiB even where the
same complete records were retained. These measurements do not justify a
memory-saving claim.

A group budget is not a cap on records, rows, memory, query time, or local
depth. These fixed-seed observations do not prove equal-inclusion behavior or
preservation of exact local density ratios. Equal inclusion is the intended
pseudorandom property; neither the contract nor correctness tests promise exact
local density ratios. Nor do they establish
navigation latency, scroll frame rates, rare-support retention, or final WGS
qualification.

## Native renderer inspection

At the chosen budget, the actual
[ordinary 10 kb renderer](../images/sampling-ordinary-10000.png) and
[deeper 10 kb renderer](../images/sampling-deeper-10000.png) retain recognizable
bodies, strand tips, clips, and insertion markers at the previously measured
scale. The [deeper 1 kb capture](../images/sampling-deeper-1000.png) retains the
larger bodies and colored clips expected at that width. These are renderer-only
captures of the top visible rows, not whole-window or full-depth screenshots.
The 500-group deeper 10 kb capture had visibly fewer occupied rows; the
5,000-group capture had a substantially deeper packed layout. Geometry alone
does not prove preservation of biological evidence.

The measurements preceded the native sampling-warning control. Separate native
UI tests demonstrate its loading precedence, amber ready state, accessible
text, keyboard focus, and restoration; these renderer images do not provide
that status-control evidence.
